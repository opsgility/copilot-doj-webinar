## 1. Executive Summary

Operation Safe Communities 2026 was a 45-day coordinated multi-agency law enforcement initiative targeting organized violent crime networks and fentanyl distribution organizations operating across 12 major metropolitan areas. Led by the FBI with active participation from the Drug Enforcement Administration (DEA), Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives (ATF), and U.S. Marshals Service (USMS), the operation achieved significant disruption of targeted criminal networks and produced measurable results across all priority enforcement areas.

The operation resulted in **342 federal arrests**, seizure of **1,847 illegal firearms**, interdiction of **approximately 89 kilograms of fentanyl**, and forfeiture of **$23.4 million in criminal proceeds**. State and local law enforcement partners contributed 2,100 personnel across 47 participating agencies.

Operation Safe Communities 2026 demonstrated the effectiveness of the DOJ's coordinated enforcement model while also revealing systemic coordination gaps that must be addressed in future multi-agency operations.

## 4. What Went Well

### 4.1 Interagency Coordination Framework

The pre-operation Joint Coordination Protocol (JCP) — established at a three-day planning conference in December 2025 — provided clear command relationships, deconfliction procedures, and communication standards that proved effective throughout the operation. Participating agencies reported high satisfaction with pre-operation communication and clarity of roles.

> *"The December planning conference was the most important investment we made. Every agency knew the lanes. Deconfliction worked because we built the relationships before the operation began."*  
> — Special Agent in Charge, FBI Atlanta Field Office

### 4.2 Rapid Prosecutorial Integration

Embedding Assistant U.S. Attorneys (AUSAs) within each city's strike team from day one enabled same-day review of arrest packages and significantly reduced the time from arrest to charging decision. This model is recommended for all future multi-agency surge operations.

### 4.3 Fentanyl Interdiction Results

DEA's Operation Scoreboard database, used for the first time in a coordinated surge operation, enabled real-time tracking of drug seizure quantities and supply chain indicators across all 12 cities. DEA analysts credited the tool with identifying a previously unknown distribution hub in Phoenix that yielded the operation's largest single fentanyl seizure (22.3 kg).

### 4.4 State and Local Partner Engagement

The 47 participating state and local agencies demonstrated strong operational discipline and adherence to federal evidentiary standards. Pre-operation training conducted by FBI and DEA in November-December 2025 was cited as the primary reason for this outcome.

## 5. Areas for Improvement

### 5.1 Information Sharing Delays

**Observation:** Case-level information sharing between FBI and ATF was delayed by an average of 36 hours due to incompatible case management systems. In three instances, this delay resulted in near-simultaneous investigative contact with the same target by different agencies.

**Root Cause:** ATF's N-Force case management system does not interface directly with FBI's Sentinel platform. Liaison agents exchanging information by telephone created an avoidable bottleneck.

**Recommendation:** Prioritize development of a read-only Sentinel-to-N-Force data bridge or establish a shared operational database for future joint operations. DOJ CIO should lead a 90-day technical assessment.

### 5.2 Fugitive Apprehension Shortfall

**Observation:** The operation apprehended 38 of the 45 targeted high-priority fugitives (84%), falling short of the 100% target. Seven targets were not located within the operational window.

**Root Cause:** Three targets had relocated out of the 12 target cities in the 30-60 day period before execution. The fugitive target list was frozen at D-45 and not updated. Four others had moved to locations outside USMS district boundaries without prior coordination.

**Recommendation:** Establish a live target validation protocol requiring weekly location confirmation for all Tier 1 fugitive targets beginning D-30. Coordinate district boundary exceptions with USMS HQ in advance.

### 5.3 Overcrowding at Federal Detention Facilities

**Observation:** The volume of arrests in Chicago and Los Angeles exceeded initial projections by 28% and 22%, respectively. Both cities' U.S. Marshal-managed detention facilities reached temporary overcapacity on arrest surge days, creating processing delays of 8-12 hours.

**Root Cause:** Detention capacity was allocated based on conservative arrest targets. BOP and USMS were not fully integrated into surge-day logistics planning.

**Recommendation:** Future operations should include a BOP/USMS detention capacity review as a mandatory planning element, with contingency agreements pre-negotiated with county and state facilities.

### 5.4 Public Communications Coordination

**Observation:** Three of the 12 U.S. Attorneys' Offices issued press releases on arrest days before the DOJ Office of Public Affairs (OPA) issued the national release. This created inconsistent messaging in local markets and one premature disclosure of an ongoing investigative technique.

**Root Cause:** The Public Affairs Protocol annex to the JCP did not specify a mandatory hold period before local releases.

**Recommendation:** All future multi-agency surge operations should include a minimum 4-hour hold after OPA national release before local district press releases are authorized. OPA should issue template press release language to all districts 48 hours before execution.

## 6. Key Observations and Lessons Learned

| # | Observation | Priority | Recommended Action |
|---|-------------|----------|--------------------|
| 1 | Joint coordination protocols established before the operation were the single greatest contributor to operational success | High | Mandate pre-operation planning conferences (minimum 3 days) for all future multi-city operations |
| 2 | AUSA embedding in strike teams dramatically accelerated charging timelines | High | Institutionalize embedded prosecutorial support as standard practice |
| 3 | Case management system incompatibility between FBI and ATF created unnecessary delays and deconfliction risks | High | DOJ CIO to assess technical integration options within 90 days |
| 4 | Detention capacity planning was insufficient for upper-bound arrest scenarios | Medium | Require BOP/USMS detention capacity review in all future operation planning cycles |
| 5 | Fugitive target lists must be validated continuously, not frozen at the planning horizon | Medium | Implement weekly target validation requirement beginning D-30 |
| 6 | Public affairs coordination protocols were insufficient | Medium | Update JCP template to include mandatory OPA pre-release hold requirement |
| 7 | State and local partners performed at a high level when pre-operation training was provided | Low | Continue and expand pre-operation training partnerships with state and local agencies |

---

## 7. Recommendations for Future Operations

1. **Establish a DOJ Multi-Agency Surge Operations Standing Protocol** that incorporates the lessons of OSC-26 into a reusable planning framework.
2. **Invest in shared operational technology** that enables real-time deconfliction across FBI, DEA, ATF, and USMS case management systems.
3. **Institutionalize embedded AUSA models** in all future joint enforcement surges.
4. **Require detention capacity analysis** as a mandatory annex to all multi-city operations with projected arrests exceeding 100.
5. **Create a National Press Release Coordination Cell** within OPA for future multi-city operations to ensure consistent and timely public communication.

---

## 8. Conclusion

Operation Safe Communities 2026 achieved its primary enforcement objectives and demonstrated that coordinated multi-agency DOJ operations can produce significant results when supported by rigorous pre-operation planning, clear command relationships, and embedded prosecutorial support. The operation's shortfalls were not failures of personnel or mission focus — they were systemic coordination and technology gaps that can be addressed through the recommendations in this report.

The Deputy Attorney General is requested to approve the formation of a DOJ Multi-Agency Operations Working Group to develop a standing protocol based on the lessons of OSC-26, to be ready for implementation by October 2026.
